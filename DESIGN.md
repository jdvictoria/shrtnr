# Design System: shrten Dispatch Label and Ledger

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Dispatch Ledger"**

shrten treats a URL as a dispatched item with a traceable operational life. The public Dispatch Label remains the first expression: a long destination enters through one broad intake field and leaves as a compact link ready to hand off. The Dispatch Ledger extends that same physical language through access, management, analytics, collaboration, and route resolution without asking users to learn a second interface.

Warm thermal stock and carbon rules make the system tactile without making it ornamental. Safety orange marks the one action that commits the current workflow, while cobalt behaves like registration ink for routes, selected state, keyboard focus, metrics, and completed handoffs. Antonio carries promises, titles, and high-signal figures; Outfit carries readable product content; monospace labels expose operational structure.

The system is flat, square, and task-dense. Public perforation belongs to a real intake-to-handoff seam, auth becomes a ruled access ticket, signed-in work uses a persistent dispatch index plus dense ledger, analytics preserves the same compact reading order, and terminal routes become status documents. The metaphor clarifies the work and never changes the product's behavior.

**Key Characteristics:**

- Warm, lightly mottled thermal paper in light mode and deep-navy stock in dark mode.
- Carbon-black structure, cobalt registration cues, and one safety-orange commitment action per surface.
- Antonio display and metric roles, Outfit body copy, and monospace operational labels.
- Square controls, ruled headers, semantic summaries, dense ledger rows, and flat document overlays.
- Restrained perforation only where access, routing, or handoff creates a real seam.
- One operating language from anonymous shortening through account work and recipient status.

## Colors

The palette behaves like a restricted two-ink print job on thermal stock: orange commits, cobalt routes and reports, and carbon ink structures.

### Primary

- **Safety Orange** (`dispatch-orange`; `night-orange` in dark mode): reserved for the highest-commitment action in the current surface, such as shortening, account access, team creation, or returning from a terminal route.

### Secondary

- **Registration Cobalt** (`dispatch-cobalt`; `night-cobalt` in dark mode): marks routes, registration targets, selected navigation, focus, disclosure state, copied handoffs, primary data bars, maps, and high-signal figures.
- **Verified Green** (`success`; `night-success` in dark mode): communicates completed availability, joins, uploads, and copy confirmation while maintaining WCAG AA text contrast on both paper stocks.
- **Folder Cyan** (`folder-default`): the persisted default identifier for newly created folders; user-selected folder and tag colors remain data, not interface accents.

### Neutral

- **Thermal Paper / Deep Navy Stock** (`dispatch-paper`; `night-paper` in dark mode): the page stock, access ticket, ledger panel, and overlay surface.
- **Carbon Ink** (`dispatch-ink`; `night-ink` in dark mode): text, outlines, dividers, and structural rules.
- **Faded Receipt Ink** (`dispatch-muted`; `night-muted` in dark mode): placeholders, summaries, hints, secondary metadata, and quiet chart labels.
- **Dispatch Rule** (`dispatch-rule`; `night-dispatch-rule` in dark mode): the stronger dashed seam used by the public Label.
- **Ledger Rule** (`ledger-rule`; `night-ledger-rule` in dark mode): the quieter dashed divider used inside auth and management documents.
- **Copy White** (`copy-white`): copy-button text and other foreground content over cobalt only.

**The One Orange Action Rule.** Safety orange marks the highest-commitment action in the current surface; when an auth or route-status action becomes orange, the public navbar entry is demoted to carbon ink so two orange commitments never compete.

**The Blue Routing Rule.** Cobalt marks direction, registration, selected state, keyboard focus, data, or a completed handoff; it does not compete with orange for commitment priority.

**The Quantitative Gradient Exception.** Decorative gradients are absent; a continuous cobalt scale is allowed only when it encodes quantitative analytics, such as the country-map legend.

## Typography

**Display Font:** Antonio (with sans-serif fallback)

**Body Font:** Outfit (with sans-serif fallback)

**Label/Mono Font:** the platform monospace stack

**Character:** Antonio compresses large statements, document titles, and figures into the footprint of shipping typography. Outfit keeps tables and explanations readable at operational density, while the monospace stack makes labels, URLs, controls, hints, codes, and status metadata read like accountable records.

### Hierarchy

- **Public Display** (`display`): the oversized homepage promise, tightly tracked at responsive hero scale; small screens reduce it to `clamp(3.1rem, 14.5vw, 4rem)`.
- **Auth Display** (`auth-display`): the access-side proposition, capped to a narrow measure so it reads like the heading on a large admission document.
- **Status Title** (`status-title`): the oversized expired or missing-route headline inside a centered terminal document.
- **Page Title** (`page-title`): dashboard, team, and analytics document headers; at 640px and below it resolves to 3.3rem.
- **Ticket Title** (`ticket-title`): access tickets, protected-link tickets, dialogs, and ledger-panel titles.
- **Metric** (`metric`): cobalt figures in ruled summaries; stat-strip variants may increase to 2.7rem while preserving Antonio weight 500 and line-height 1.
- **Action** (`action`): the public 98px shorten control only; do not use it for ordinary management actions.
- **Body** (`body`): tables, descriptions, and longer explanatory copy. Keep prose quiet and generally below 60ch on auth and status surfaces.
- **Label** (`label`): public fields, disclosures, and URLs. Preserve natural case unless the control is explicitly stamped as operational metadata.
- **Code** (`code`): uppercased route codes, page codes, access labels, and summary definitions.
- **Index Label** (`index-label`): sidebar group labels and the compact Dispatch Ledger marker.
- **Micro** (`micro`): keycaps, footer metadata, compact tags, table headings, badges, and copy controls.
- **Responsive Variants** (`display-mobile`, `auth-display-mobile`, `page-title-mobile`, `wordmark-mobile`): the exact compact scales used when the document reflows below its content-driven breakpoints.
- **Operational Variants** (`body-lead`, `label-fluid`, `action-responsive`, `result-link`, `supporting`, `compact`, `compact-strong`, `micro-control`, `calendar-label`, `index-small`): the documented dense-control and explanatory steps already present across public, auth, and ledger surfaces.

**The Three-Voice Rule.** Antonio persuades and announces, monospace operates, and Outfit explains; never introduce a fourth typographic voice without a product-level reason.

**The Ledger Density Rule.** Dense does not mean tiny: use the compact monospace roles for labels and metadata, keep data values and actions legible, and let rules—not reduced type—create structure.

## Layout

The public page remains an edge-to-edge operating sheet rather than a centered card. Its 76px sticky navigation leads into the 56% / 44% hero, a full-width destination band, and the `minmax(300px, 40%) 68px minmax(0, 1fr)` options-to-result handoff. At 900px and below the public hero, destination action, and lower handoff stack; the perforation channel rotates into a horizontal tear line. At 540px and below, public insets settle at 18px and the result content stacks without removing any function.

The signed-in shell uses a persistent 16rem dispatch index that can collapse to a 3rem icon rail. The index keeps All Links, Archived, Teams, Folders, Tags, theme, and sign-out in one ruled column; its selected row is cobalt and receives a square registration mark. Below 768px the index becomes a 16rem left sheet and a 56px mobile bar provides the trigger and current Dispatch Ledger context.

Ledger workspaces use a fluid `clamp(24px, 4vw, 58px)` inset. General link management may use the full available sheet; analytics and team surfaces cap the ledger at 1180px, while focused intake caps at 720px. Each workspace begins with a full-bleed ruled document header, followed by semantic summary bands and then task-dense panels, tables, charts, or team rows.

Auth uses a two-column access document: a larger context panel and a ticket column with a minimum 440px track. At 900px and below it becomes one column and the vertical dashed handoff becomes a horizontal seam. Status routes center a single document up to 650px wide. At 640px and below, workspace gutters become 16px, every page header explicitly stacks its title and actions, summary bands become one column, stat-strip rules rotate from vertical to horizontal, and auth/status padding contracts without removing controls.

Responsive changes are reflow, not subtraction. Destination, advanced options, result, copy, theme, account access, filters, table actions, analytics ranges, team permissions, and recovery actions remain available at supported widths; dense link tables become ruled mobile records below 640px.

## Elevation & Depth

Public, auth, ledger, chart, and status surfaces are flat at rest. Depth comes from the repeating 334px × 376px paper plate, translucent paper-on-paper fills, solid and dashed rules, clipped or perforated edges, and the light/dark stock change. Dialogs and the mobile navigation sheet are the only lifted documents: the backdrop darkens the underlying sheet, and a conventional shadow may reinforce the temporary overlay boundary.

### Shadow Vocabulary

- **Dialog Lift** (`0 1px 3px 0 rgb(0 0 0 / 0.1), 0 1px 2px -1px rgb(0 0 0 / 0.1)`): optional on modal documents opened above the ledger.
- **Sheet Lift** (`0 20px 25px -5px rgb(0 0 0 / 0.1), 0 8px 10px -6px rgb(0 0 0 / 0.1)`): mobile dispatch index only, paired with the 60% black sheet overlay.

**The Carbon Rule.** Resting hierarchy comes from one-pixel ink rules, perforations, and tonal stock—not ambient drop shadows, blurred glows, or glass panels.

**The Overlay Exception Rule.** Shadow may distinguish a temporary modal or mobile sheet from the document below; it must disappear when the overlay closes and must not migrate onto ordinary cards.

## Shapes

Transactional controls, tabs, badges, cards, tables, menus, popovers, dialogs, selects, and tooltips are square. Ticket identity comes from line work: solid outer frames, dashed seams, circular perforation holes, 12px notches, clipped result corners, fine cobalt routing lines, crosshair registration targets, and compact barcode ticks. Circles are reserved for registration and perforation mechanics, quantitative chart marks, configured folder/tag dots, and real account avatars.

Status documents use repeating side perforations because they are terminal route handoffs. Access uses one dashed transition and a registration circle between context and credentials. Ledger panels do not inherit these perforations: they use ruled headers, rows, and summary partitions instead.

**The Square Control Rule.** Inputs and action buttons meet their ledger geometry with zero radius; rounding them into pills or soft cards breaks the dispatch metaphor and weakens the operational hierarchy.

**The Real Handoff Rule.** Use perforation only where the workflow crosses access, routing, issuance, or terminal resolution; ordinary grouping uses carbon rules.

## Components

### Navigation and Action Hierarchy

The public navigation remains a 76px ruled header with brand and inverse 48px theme square on the left and account state on the right. On the public creation surface, anonymous `Get started` is the full-height orange entry. On auth and route-status surfaces the in-page commitment becomes orange and `Get started` becomes carbon ink on paper. Signed-in account controls retain their current dashboard, team, and sign-out destinations.

The dashboard replaces the public header with the persistent dispatch index. Its brand block, group labels, active states, counts, inline folder/tag creation, theme control, and sign-out remain in one collapsible column; mobile uses the ruled top bar and sheet version of the same index.

### Destination Band, Options Ticket, and Result Handoff

The signature public input remains a 98px two-pixel address field joined to the 98px orange action. Cobalt connects label to destination and supplies the focus outline. The options disclosure previews alias, password, expiration, and geographic redirects as ruled rows before revealing the functional fields. The detachable result tray keeps quiet cobalt writing rules, a full copied value, an `aria-live` update, and the two-second `Copied` acknowledgement.

At small widths, the destination field becomes 78px high and the action 66px high within one stacked frame. The center perforation rotates rather than disappearing, and long result links truncate visually without changing the copied value.

### Access Ticket

Sign-in and sign-up pair a contextual document with a 560px maximum credential ticket. Antonio states the account purpose, a cobalt access code identifies the operator path, a numbered monospace list explains the workflow, and the ruled ticket leads directly to email-and-password access. Inputs are at least 52px high; labels are uppercased monospace; the local submit is the one orange commitment. Switching between sign-in and sign-up stays below a dashed rule.

### Route-Status Documents

Expired and missing routes use the same centered 650px status document with a cobalt line icon, Antonio status headline, explicit `ROUTE STATUS / 410` or `ROUTE STATUS / 404` code, concise recovery copy, side perforations, and one orange return action. Protected links use the access-ticket variant: lock registration tile, `ROUTE / PROTECTED HANDOFF` code, password field, inline error, pending state, and orange Continue action.

### Page Headers and Semantic Summaries

Every management surface begins with a full-bleed ruled header. The header pairs an Antonio page title with a cobalt monospace page code such as `LINK OPERATIONS / PERSONAL`, `LINK ANALYTICS`, `SHARED OPERATIONS`, `TEAM LEDGER`, or `WORKSPACE INTAKE`; identity, route, date, role, and task actions sit below or opposite without competing with the title.

Operational totals use semantic `dl`, `dt`, and `dd` markup. The summary band contains two or three equal ruled cells: uppercased monospace definitions and cobalt Antonio values. These summaries stack into full-width rows at 640px and below.

### Ledger Panels, Tables, and Mobile Records

Ledger panels are translucent square documents with carbon outlines and ruled headers. Link management keeps search, date filters, sorting, pagination, status, metadata, and row actions within one panel. Desktop uses a dense table with monospace headings and Outfit values; below 640px each row becomes a bordered record preserving slug, destination, notes, tags, folder, clicks, state, expiry, date, and actions.

Pinned rows receive a quiet cobalt tint and registration cue. Cobalt may show selected filters, links, counts, and focus; destructive actions retain their explicit destructive color. Empty states stay inside the same ledger panel rather than becoming a disconnected illustration or promotional card.

### Analytics Adaptation

Analytics remains task-dense: the route header and two-cell semantic summary lead to a ruled click chart, compact 7d/30d controls, and square tabs for country, device, browser, OS, and referrer views. Bars and active tabs use cobalt; axes and gridlines use muted ink and dashed carbon rules. Tooltips are square bordered documents. Donut segments and the country-map scale use the cobalt tonal ramp; the only gradient is the quantitative legend.

### Team Adaptation

The teams index uses a continuous ruled ledger of clickable rows with name, slug, role, member count, and link count. Team detail preserves the same document header, tabs, ledger panels, bordered member and invitation rows, square role selects, permission-gated controls, and copied-invite feedback. Team creation is a narrow workspace-intake document whose Create Team button becomes the one orange commitment.

### Shared Square Controls and Overlays

Default, outline, secondary, ghost, destructive, link, compact, and icon buttons share square borders and visible focus. Inputs, textareas, selects, tabs, badges, tables, calendars, dropdowns, popovers, dialogs, tooltips, and sheets use the same zero-radius control language. Icon actions are 28–36px squares inside already-generous rows or headers; destructive meanings must retain text, accessible names, or tooltips.

Modal documents use an 80% black dialog backdrop, a square bordered paper surface, Antonio title, and 200ms fade/zoom/slide state changes. Mobile index sheets use a 60% black backdrop and 300ms horizontal travel. Dropdowns, popovers, selects, and tooltips are compact square overlays with carbon borders and cobalt active or focus state.

### Motion and Reduced Motion

Public result arrival uses 360ms `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.8, 0.2, 1)`. Directional arrow shifts use 180ms ease, footer-link lifts use 150ms ease, the sidebar width uses 200ms linear motion, modal documents use 200ms state motion, and the mobile sheet uses 300ms ease-in-out. Motion communicates direction, progress, state change, or acknowledgement; it never loops except for pending spinners. Public directional and result motion is removed under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`.

**The Motion Has a Job Rule.** Movement may show travel, access, progress, selection, or confirmation; if it does not explain one of those states, keep the printed surface still.

### Accessibility Requirements

Preserve semantic labels, semantic summary lists, button elements, table structure, tab relationships, live result announcements, changing copy labels, explicit password visibility labels, visible cobalt keyboard focus, disabled-state communication, and light/dark contrast. Decorative planes, seams, barcodes, perforations, and registration marks remain hidden from assistive technology. Keep touch targets generous, preserve full task access in the mobile records and sheet, and keep auth/status copy concise.

### Behavior Preservation

The visual system does not redefine product behavior. Anonymous shortening, URL validation, the 400ms alias availability check, custom alias rules, password protection, future-only expiration, geographic redirects, pending state, error/success toasts, result reset, copy behavior, theme switching, authentication redirects and callback URLs, link filtering/sorting/pagination, folder and tag actions, analytics ranges, team roles and permissions, invitations, and all existing destinations remain intact.

**The Behavior Lock Rule.** Visual extensions may rearrange presentation responsively, but they must not change shortening rules, API contracts, persistence, permissions, account requirements, authentication behavior, or navigation destinations.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:

- **Do** make each operating sequence read left-to-right or top-to-bottom: intake, optional routing, commitment, result, and next action.
